Your champion, the one you've been rooting for since you first started watching gets brutally planted onto the floor by his opponent and pinned. They look like they've been hit by a truck, surely they are knocked out, unconscious, however you wish to put it. Agonisingly the referee slides over and begins his count with the crowds echoing his words "1... 2...", time slows down and you think surely this is it. Just before the hand slaps the canvas to end this brawl your hero shows that there is still life within them and raises their shoulder from the matt causing an eruption of noise as the crowd goes wild for this close call, with you joining in just as extatically as everyone else.

This moment, which feels so special to those watching, is a common occurance to those who watch professional wrestling. It's the culmination of the viewers investment into the unfolding narrative which swerves their expectations causing an explosion of emotion from them, it is known as a "kickout".

It creates that shock and excitement that makes wrestling so memorable.
In this psuedo-sport, pseudo-theatre hybrid the most common rule in a matchup is that the first one to pin their opponent with both shoulders on the ring canvas for three consecutive seconds under a referee's count is the winner of a fight. For the majority of matches, this rule is what they revolve around, damaging the opponent/opponents enough so that when a pin attempt is made the audience is convinced that this may be the conclusion of the match. Most of the time, this occurs after a wrestler hits their "Finisher", their signature move which is widely recognised as their most powerfulest. A kickout, which is when the wrestler raises their shoulders before the referee counts to three can be used to swerve the audiences expectations when they truly believe a match is about to finish. At it's best it can define wrestling history as the buildup makes the moment significant to the viewer, at it's worst it can destroy a wrestlers persona and make the audience disengage from the product they are watching.
